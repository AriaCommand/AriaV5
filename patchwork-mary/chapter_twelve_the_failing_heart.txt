“Mary!” Gillian’s voice cut through the empty rehearsal hall. “You’re drifting again. The lift comes after the turn, not before.” 
It was only a few days since Madame Marionette and Baron Barbossa had announced to all of Aurea about the joint performance between Gillian Rose and Patchwork Mary. Whether Gillian knew it or not, Mary knew the Madame well enough to know that this performance would be more or less settling a score and setting the record straight on who was the true star of Aurea. So Mary’s dismay, practice occurred not long after the announcement. Though it was only through practicing together did Mary realize that Gillian may be every bit of the practice perfectionist as her aunt. 
Mary lowered her hand quickly, forcing a smile. “Sorry, I don’t know what happened there. Maybe I’m just off on the timing.”
“The timing is perfect when you actually do it.” Gillian crossed the floor, her boots clicking against the polished wood of the Golden Aurea stage. She stopped in front of Mary, arms crossed, assessing. “You been off all morning. And you’re looking a bit pale. Are you okay?”
Mary’s eyes flickered at the pale comment. She held her breath and thought back to what Rufus taught her way back. In the same way girls listen to bird songs, Mary listened to the steady rhythm of her chest. The tightness in her ribs was the first thing she noticed., a cold that had nothing to do with the draft  creeping through the theater. She pressed a palm to her chest, feeling the faint mechanical rhythm of her chest that had been her constant companion since he first installed the pacemaker. 
Tick. Tick. Tick. 
Pause. 
Tick. 
“No,” Mary responded back. Her emerald eyes wide. She rose up, but felt a sudden lance of chilling pain through her chest, stealing all her words. She gasped. For one terrible second, she felt nothing at all. Just silence where her heart lie in the pacemaker. 
“Mary?” Gillian’s voice changed. The sharp instructors edge had soften into something softer. “What is it?”
“Nothing,” Mary breathed. She straighten, smoothing her rehearsal skirt with trembling fingers. “Just a chill. These old theaters, you know how they are. “
“Mary are you sure?”
Another jolt of pain. This one more striking than the last. 
“No,” Mary coughed. It was like someone was stepping on her chest. “Okay, I need to see my doctor.”
Mary didn’t even wait for permission. She broke out in a sprint out of the rehearsal hall. The frost followed her. It traced her footsteps in the hallways. It bloomed on the door handle of her personal suite. She grasped onto the chilled doorhandle for balance before collapsing at the door. Luckily, Rufus was talking to the Madame inside when she arrived. 
Mary’s eyes were loosely focused as she felt hands surround her. Rufus yelled something to Madame who rushed out and closed the door behind them. 
“Rufus,” Mary said. 
“It’s okay,” Rufus said. He began shushing her as she felt a cold stethoscope over her chest. “Just shut up and live.”
He did a full workup on her body before she felt him working on her chest. The haziness slowly focused as he buttoned up her blouse. She looked down and noticed the scar on her chest had spread. 
Rufus’s finger hovered over it, trembling. “When did it start?”
“The pain?” Mary said breathless. 
“No the chills,” Rufus said. 
Mary closed her eyes recalling the details. 
“Maybe a few weeks ago,” She said. 
“Mary!” Rufus said. He had a hand on his head. 
“I thought I was just tired or cold—” Mary swallowed, the motion painful in her tight throat. “I thought it was just from practice and all the performances.”
“Am I going to be okay for the performance, Rufus?” Mary breathed. 
“The hell with the performance!” Rufus said. “The pacemaker is failing Mary. All these acts and stunts are pushing it to its limits. We both knew it wasn’t a permanent solution. You need to stop.”
“No!” Mary exclaimed. She bolted upright and patted her chest before giving Rufus a toothy grin. “I’m fine! I’m going to perform with Gillian. I need to.”
“It’s getting to dangerous, Mary,” Rufus said. “I can’t send you out like this. Mary—your Grimm body, it keeps rejecting the mechanism. The frost damage is breaking your heart. I’m barely containing the cold as it is.”
“Then try harder!” Mary said. “I need to perform. I must. I need to for Madame Marionette and Aurea. They’re counting on me!”
“Not at the expense of your life!” 
“Then make the ice part of the performance!” Mary pleaded. “We can do that right?” 
Rufus held Mary in his arms.
“Mary! Listen to me. You need to give your heart to someone,” Rufus said. “You’re running out of time and the pacemaker won’t last. That is the most important thing right now. Everything else is secondary.”
“No,” Mary said. She closed her eyes feeling another jolt of cold brush over her body. She staggered to her feet. Her fingers were now blue. She proceeded to detach the blue bits and started sewing new fingers from a box she kept by her bedside. She took the sewing kit from behind her ear and began reattaching new fingers. Then as she had many times in the past, her emerald eyes glazed over to him. “Fine, then take it then, Rufus.”
Rufus looked at her and blinked and sighed before shaking his head as he always did. 
“I said no,” Rufus said. He held up his arm reveal how far the metal had crept onto it. His eyes wet behind his goggles, his lens smudged with grease and perhaps tears. “Mary please. You know I’m a dying man. Give your heart to Madame Marionette.”
Mary stared at him. 
“What?”
“She has the resource that I don’t. She’ll outlive me and you by strides,” Rufus continued. “Connections to the best surgeons in Aurea. She can—”
“Madame Marionette?” Mary’s laugh came out brittle, broken. “If you don’t want it, what makes you think she will? She never wanted…a child anyway.”
Rufus let go of her shoulders. He stepped back, running a shaking hand through his wild gray hair. When he spoke again, his voice was barely above a whisper. "Your life is worth more than applause, Mary."
Mary looked at him — really looked at him. At the man who had found her half-dead in the snow, who had built her new arms when hers had turned black with frost, who had given her a mechanical heart when her own had frozen solid in her chest. The man who had sent her away, and brought her back, and sent her away again, every time breaking both their hearts a little more.
She thought of the performance hall. Of the lights, and the music, and the moment when she and Gillian would move as one across the ice. Of Madame Marionette's face in the audience, watching her, finally seeing her as worthy of Golden Aurea.
"You don't understand," Mary said.
"Then help me understand."
“Madame needs me to perform.” She placed her hand back over her chest, feeling the stuttering rhythm beneath her palm. "Without this, without her, I'm nothing. Just a broken doll from the Wasteland. The stage is the only place I've ever felt —" She stopped. Swallowed. "Warm.”
“Mary, please. Can you please ask her at least?” Rufus said. “I can’t let you die on that stage.”
“But you can’t take my heart either,” Mary said quietly. 
The silence between them spoke volumes. It lingered in quiet acknowledgement before Mary nodded and flashed her emerald eyes back at Rufus. 
“We incorporate it into the show. When I jump in the tank, it freezes. Gillian will stab swords into it and I will escape out of it. Make it so. That will be our performance showstopper this time. I’ll—at least ask the Madame, okay? Just make it happen. If you won’t accept me or my heart, please just do this one favor for me then. Whatever modifications I need, whatever it takes to make it happen. I’m doing this performance. Please, Rufus?”
“You’ll die,” Rufus said finally. 
“Then I’llll die on stage.” Mary pushed herself upright, ignoring the way the room tilted, the way her vision spotted with darkness at the edges. "That's better than dying as a nobody. Better than dying in the Wasteland where you found me.  At least this way, someone will remember I was there."
She walked to the door. Each step sent ice crackling through her veins, but she walked anyway.
"Mary." Rufus's voice stopped her, but she didn't turn. "I lost a child once. Before you. Before any of this. I thought I was protecting her by staying away, by —" He made a sound, half laugh, half sob. "I can't lose another one. I can't watch another daughter choose death over —"
"I'm not your daughter," Mary said to the door.
"I know," Rufus whispered. "That's what makes it worse."

# Chapter Sixteen — Cold Curtain

*Draft 1 — 2026-05-21*

The doors opened at seven. Just enough time to catch dinner and make it to the performance. From backstage, the guests looked like dark ants filling the seats. Mary peeked between the curtains, and gave an involuntary shiver realizing this will be the last time she'd feel the nerves before a performance.

Unlike Gillian, Mary's outfit for this show was less extravagant. She was wearing a black suit and white dress shirt underneath. The baron actually had a good idea to use Gillian's beauty and presence to draw the audience eyes when Mary would do something magical.

"You know, take this to heart, but I believe a Great Pretender's greatest trick in their bag should be a beautiful partner on stage. You might find the trick goes off easier than you'd expect knowing you got something pretty to look at," he'd said.

Whether that was the case or not, Mary would find out one way or another. Another compromise that Mary and Madame Marionette made for this performance was that it would have a narrative flow to it unlike Mary's previous performances where she'd perform fantastic feats one after another.

The performance itself would be based off an old tale from the coastal town of Muse. Mary would play the part of the artist, while Gillian would be the fairy that would dance around whispering good ideas to her. The story would show how Gillian the fairy would inspire more and more wild inspirations for Mary's character that culminate with the ice block escape at the end.

Pose. Prestige. Prestidigitation. Mary repeated these words in her head before stepping out under the spotlight. She exhaled and flashed a wide toothy grin at the audience. Just like that. She went from Mary to Patchwork Mary.

"Ladies and gentleman! Thank you for coming to this joint performance between the Golden Aurea and Balthazar's Gate. We will be doing a rather unique rendition of an old fairy tale about an artist and his fairy." Every movement Mary made was measured and precise. She moved as if she was strings in perfect sync with the audience. She gave a stylistic pause letting her words linger into the stillness of the theater before giving a final bow. "Enjoy!"

Though unlike the other performances, Mary felt colder than normal. She couldn't quite place it because she could actively feel her pacemaker getting weaker as the night went on or if for the first time she lonely on stage despite being cheered on by thousands of guests who all came to see her and Gillian.

The first few performances almost felt like a blur to Mary as if she were just going through the motions while at the same time watching herself perform everything perfectly and with pose as she went through all her best tricks. The bullet catch, the shadow cutting, and the various lower skilled sleight of hand parlor tricks she'd pepper the performance with as the music and Gillian danced around to tell the story.

Tick. Tick. Tick.

Mary felt a chill fill her lungs and exhaled with a cold breath. Suddenly she was bowing to the crowd. The abrupt sound of roaring applause snapped her back to the moment. Their cheers their roars as she'd just done…she couldn't remember. All she could see is the blurred sea of shadowy faces for as far as she could see. She imagined Madame Marionette was amongst them, but thinking of her made her heart hurt, so Mary shook the thought away quickly and turned toward Gillian who was smiling and panting after their last act.

But the show was not over yet.

Under the harsh glare of the spotlight, Patchwork Mary stepped on stage. The hushed whispers of the audience ebbed and flowed into an eerie hush as the spotlight beamed down exposing piercing green eyes and a bright toothy smile on a face webbed with scars. Her hands remained bound behind her back. Next to her was a ladder leading at least two stories to a small platform, and below it, a large tank of water that she calibrated to self lock upon receiving the exact weight threshold of herself and the water.

Patchwork Mary smiled and waited just long enough until the audience can get their applause in. They all paid for the show, so it wasn't like the promise of her performance wasn't guaranteed. Yet, she played the game of humbly bowing and smiling, while her leg grew numb from the new weight from her latest contraption hidden within her performance outfit. The audience saw art. Mary felt braces that would make her performance perfect.

And it was perfect.

In a single jump, Mary ignored the ladder entirely and leaped onto the second story platform. She landed in silence, then sound. Always silence first, then the roar. A brace in her leg screamed. She winced, but quickly bit her tongue and revealed another toothy grin. The audience screamed louder. Blood in the boot, bleeding tongue, but perfect form —that was the bargain. One more scar for one more roar. She'd patch it up later. With that, she free fall backwards into the tank. Everything in the world disappeared in that moment. She heard nothing as she fell. Then water. The light of the ceiling above her fading into a watery hue. The tank closed as planned as she sank deeper and deeper into the water.

That's when she saw it. A screw. A loose screw had escaped a joint when she hit the water. To what that screw went to? Mary had no idea. The irony that her life could end on a loose screw was beyond her, but she knew she'd troubleshoot this and still escape. Because Patchwork Mary never missed.

Mary's hand reached for the screw. Her first attempt missed just barely, but her next reach grabbed the screw. She knew she could hold her breath for a bit. She could figure this out. She could find where the screw went. She eyed the encroaching ice that started to encase the water around her. Though the funny thing is she didn't feel cold. Just numb as she did a quick scan of where the loose screw could have come from.

Then she found it. The pacemaker.

A bubble escaped her mouth underwater at the moment. For if she was above water, those words would have said, "Fuck."

What happened next would forever cement Patchwork Mary's legend with the irony being Patchwork Mary not even being conscious of it. As a flash of cold air burst through the theater. The tank collapse as planned, but the ice splintered into a jagged icicle on stage while the rest of the Golden Aurea was blanketed by a thick layer of frost. At first people cheered, they even appreciated the realism of the performance. Even Gillian tried to salvage the moment until she noticed, like everyone else in the audience, Mary was unconscious and a large dark specter emerged out of Mary. It gave an ear piercing scream that signaled the panic in the audience.

Immediately, everyone started to rush toward the doors as the specter floated around the theater room almost as if looking for someone then left the theater hall flying out. Mary was still encased in the ice. The backstage staff along with Gillian all rushed to break Mary out of the icicle, finally breaking out the unconscious Mary. They say she was ice cold to the touch and had no heart beat. Madame Marionette eventually made her way to Mary on stage. The panic and the chaos of it all would forever be told in history as Patchwork Mary's cold curtain and the rise of the true legacy of Patchwork Mary.

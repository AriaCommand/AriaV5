History would primarily talk about Patchwork Mary in two halves.
The first focusing on her early career as the first Great Pretender of the continent. She paved the way for performances that looked magical and brought wonder, while the mechanics of the performances were more logical than people expected. For as brief as her career was, her impact was something history would talk for ages to come. Paving the way even for a certain actual witch one day who would do just the opposite of what Patchwork Mary had done on stage many years ago.
Others would talk about Patchwork Mary in a completely different light. They'd talk about the monster she became. They'd talk about how she took things too far with her performances to the point of actually using real magic to transform herself into something that still haunts the Wasteland to this day. No one lives in the Wasteland these days for Bloody Mary still lives and lingers scouring the waste for things to become a better and brighter much like the real Mary.
So for as many threads that exist describing Patchwork Mary's legend, her tale can be told in a number of ways. For Patchwork Mary did die in the Wasteland that day. That night was Mary's last performance as well. All factually correct.
However, the truth is always far stranger than reality.
"You sure you want to leave Aurea?" Madame Marionette said. She looked longingly out the window overlooking the streets of Aurea. She sipped her tea with a sigh. "It actually is nice to have you around, you know?"
"I don't have a home anymore, Chateau," Rufus said with a chuckle. He nodded toward the window where droves of dirty looking people were coming into the city. "Refugees from the Wasteland are coming in more and more each day. And I couldn't put you out to keep housing me."
"But—" Madame Marionette said. She put down her tea and looked over at Rufus. He still had the course scruffiness of a beard that had traces of white in it, while she realized the wrinkles on her arms. They had both grown old, but in that moment, she was young and looking at her then husband. "I don't want you to go."
"I'll be back," Rufus said. He walked over and gave Madame Marionette a hug. Normally, she'd have protested, but instead leaned into the gesture. He then finished by saying, "we'll be back."
"But why do you even have to leave in the first place?" Madame Marionette said. She backed away and gestured toward the streets. "You can practically blend in with all the refugees coming in these days. I don't see why you two need to leave Aurea all together."
"Mary is a household name that people think terrorizes the Wasteland now. We'll travel around until like most gossip and news, people forget about her. You know how things go around here, Chateau," Rufus said.
The door barged opened where a young woman in a long cloak came in. No bandages, but still with the emerald green eyes and a large toothy grin on her face.
"I couldn't stop from overhearing," Mary blurted out. "But dad's right. Mom, I'll be back soon. Don't worry! We'll be back once the people move onto the next shiny performance that comes along. Probably orchestrated by you!"
The words sparked a warmth in Madame Marionette. Well, rather one word Mary said. Madame Marionette sighed and smiled.
"Fine!" Madame Marionette said with a shrug. She leaned on her cane and glared down at Mary. "You know, you can come back. You being alive would definitely be considered your greatest feat yet. But then everyone would know that you're not that thing in the Wasteland. You can still perform."
Mary shook her head. She held an arm up.
"I'm no longer patchy anymore, remember?" Mary said. "I'm….human now. How? Maybe Rufus and I can find that out during our travels, but the important people know I'm alive at least." Mary stopped and laughed for a moment realizing that wasn't quite true. "Well, Gillian doesn't, but maybe one day I'll surprise her. Getting anywhere close to her now that she's become the quintessential star of Aurea in my absence would be a problem. Green eyes aren't exactly common."
"Now you're catching on," Madame Marionette said. She lay a hand on Mary's shoulder. "Fine, make sure to write. I'm your….mother. I worry about you."
"Honestly, Chateau. Sounds like you're being held at gunpoint every time you say that word," Rufus scoffed.
"It's new to me!" Madame Marionette protested. "I never wanted to be a mother, but I'm glad you're my daughter, Mary."
"Not Patchwork Mary?" Mary said. She gave Madame Marionette a quizzical look.
No," Madame Marionette said. "Just Mary is enough of a handful without the horrors of you risking your life. Write! I mean it!"
"Fine fine," Mary said waving her hand. "Rufus let's go! We're going to miss the train!"
"Okay, safe travels. Both of you," Madame Marionette said.
"Yes, yes," Rufus said. "We'll be back Chateau."
"I know."

The smoke was the first thing Mary saw as the carriage approached Rufus’s workshop. The second were the screams in the distance. Mary was the first to rush out to inspect the once workshop. Madame Marionette limped closely behind. The workshop looked as if someone, or rather, something ripped through the roof top. Bookshelves were knocked over, the blackboard was split in half, almost every inch of the workshop Mary grew up with was missing, even Rufus.
“Rufus! Rufus!” Mary called out. She began tossing bits of debris to see anything within the wreckage.
“Mary! Did you find him?” Madame Marionette called not far behind. “What in all the continent happened to this place?”
Mary didn’t answer. She kept searching until she saw Rufus’s leg. A cold sweat ran through her. She rushed over to where the leg was.
“Over here Madame!” Mary called. The two of them lifted and moved a fallen bookshelf to find Rufus passed out. Mary kept shaking him. “Rufus! Wake up!”
“Mary? Is that you, Mary? How are you still alive?” Rufus mumbled looking at Mary. His eyes focusing and unfocusing as he spoke begin hearty coughs. “Where is it?”
“Where is what?” Madame Marionette asked.
“Regina!” Rufus coughed. “It took her. We can’t let that thing exist in the Wasteland. Chateau, Chateau. I was a fool to become a Tin Man.”
“Hush. It’s fine. You…had your reasons,” Madame Marionette said holding him in her arms. “I…always knew why you left. I wish I was stronger back then.”
The ground rumbled and the screams in the distance grew louder. The three of them looked out to see the flames and screams were growing brighter and louder.
“I’ll stop it!” Mary said. She rose to her feet, but a hand grabbed hers before she could sprint out to the carnage.
“You can’t!” Madame Marionette said. Her hand intwined with Mary’s. The two exchanged a look. Almost mirroring their first encounter. So similar, yet so different at the same time. “Don’t be absurd. You can’t stop that thing. You’ll die!”
“I can try, Madame,” Mary said. “It’s my fault. All of it. That thing exists because of me!”
“Rufus! Talk some sense into her!” Madame Marionette said. Rufus only coughed as Madame Marionette eased him up. He began reattaching his leg.
“She’s right. Look what it did to the workshop,” Rufus said. “By some miracle you’re still alive. Don’t take that for granted.”
“I’m not! I just…” Mary said.
“You don’t owe this place anything, Mary,” Rufus said. “Let it burn for all we care.”
“No!” Mary said. “It’s….my home. I’m sorry I didn’t give my heart away sooner.”
Madame Marionette and Rufus exchanged a look.
“Fine,” Rufus said. “But we’re coming with you.”
“We are?” Madame Marionette said. “This is insane!”
“Fine, we can go without you,” Rufus said lazily.
“What? No! I have the carriage!” Madame Marionette said.
“Then you’ll help us?” Mary asked. She looked hopefully up at Madame Marionette who made a face before tapping her cane.
“Yes, yes. Now stop with the eyes before you give me cavities from the sweetness,” Madame Marionette said. “Come on then!”
They made their way into the carriage. The closer they got, the more carnage they saw. Shops were broken. People had ripped limbs. Walls looked as if they were bitten off, but they pressed forward until Mary saw a familiar uniform. It was at a remote part of the wasteland. It looked as if it was patrolling. Mary rushed out of the carriage.
“Regina! Look! She’s safe! I’ll get her!” Mary said. She ran toward Regina, but stopped noticing details that didn’t seem quite right. Knowing as much about anatomy as Mary did, something was off immediately. Her stance, the way she walked. However, the biggest giveaway was when Mary was close enough…Regina wasn’t even standing on the ground. She was…floating.
However, the second she noticed it. Regina’s silhouette turned revealing Regina’s face, or rather, the former face of Regina as the thing in front of Mary was her corpse being suspended. As slowly a large mechanical spider shaped woman descended behind Regina. It’s hand held Regina through metal strings as if enticing people close. It gave a piercing screech as its head snaked into vision.
Mary immediately made for a run. She ran outside the Wasteland town and out as far as she could, feeling whatever that thing was not far behind. The closer it got, the colder Mary felt. She eventually saw a cliff side.
“Okay, time for Patchwork Mary’s greatest feat yet,” She muttered under her breath. She shook her head in determination and made for the edge of the cliff. Because unlike the many times her life was in danger, this was real. She felt her chest ache. A reminder that no one wanted her heart anyway. She gave a sigh and flashed a toothy grin. In her mind, she was in front of crowd of thousands running head first into her greatest trick of them all. The crowd had gone silent. A pin could drop and no one would even hear it as she’d singlehandly stolen everyone’s breath in this run. It was that moment where what Mary did truly felt magical. It was then she was both Patchwork Mary and Mary. So she ran as fast as she could and jumped.
She heard the monster follow behind her and follow her off the cliff. However, unlike her other performances, there was no crowd. No cheers. No one roared in applause at her feat. In fact, no one would ever know what she did to save both Aurea and The Wasteland.
In truth, she was right. As she fell with her eyes closed off that cliffside, this would be Mary’s greatest and most unknown feat that no one would ever know. No history book would ever record this until now.
For the only ones who knew…were there that day as Mary felt something wrap around her leg. A cane.
“Mary! Hold on!” Rufus shouted. He and Madame Marionette each pulled on the cane attached to a rope with their good arms and good legs as Mary hung suspended not far off the cliff. Slowly, but surely they belayed Mary up on the cliffside. Rufus was practically coughing up smoke by the time Mary felt two hands grab a hold of her and up on the ledge again. The three of them panting as they laid on their backs.
“You saved me,” Mary said.
“Of course we did!” Madame Marionette snapped. “Never do that again!”
“Do you think it’s dead?” Mary said.
“Probably not,” Rufus said. His voice softer and more out of breath than theirs. “I…”
“Something’s wrong,” Madame Marionette said. “Rufus! Rufus!”
Mary’s heart sank as she saw Madame Marionette hold Rufus in her arms, shaking him. He was getting more pale and cold by the second. The realization set in. Rufus was dying.
“How is this possible?” Madame Marionette continued. “I thought Tin Men were suppose to live longer than humans!”
Mary felt the broken pacemaker in her chest. It was then that she realized it felt weaker. It was still working this whole time. But more so, it was then that Mary realized the power source Rufus used for the pacemaker was his own life force that was tied to the coal he swallowed. Every rejection to accept her heart came reeling through Mary’s mind as it all made sense.
At first, Rufus didn’t think he deserved her heart, but after creating the pacemaker he couldn’t accept it due to giving up part of his life force to make it.
“You old man. I can’t believe you did this,” Mary said softly. She said this feeling rain fall down, but it wasn’t rain she realized. It was her own tears falling down her face. “Rufus!”
Mary rushed over to Rufus’s side and shook him with Madame Marionette.
“Don’t you dare die on me!”
“Mary! You know him! Anything we can do to save him?” Madame Marionette said frantically. “Something! Anything!”
Mary blinked for a moment as an idea did spark in her mind.
“Just one,” Mary said quietly. She took a deep breath and ripped into her own chest. Surprisingly it didn’t hurt as much as she thought it would. She crushed the pacemaker with her hands until she held broken shards of a ruby in her hand. They still glowed with a deep red hue still. “It’s not much, but it’s all I can give.”
“Mary…” Madame Marionette said, but stopped herself.
“I love you both,” Mary said. She then thrusted the pieces of the rubies into Rufus’s chest. Then…everything went black for Mary. The world that’d been her stage came to a close. She fell onto the ground in front of her two biggest fans that she had on the continent.
It was at this moment…Patchwork Mary took her final bow.

# Patchwork Mary — Deep Notes

## Status
In development. Prequel to *The Vampire of Maple Town*.

## Timeline
Exists **long before** Charlie and Alice were born. Patchwork Mary was the first Great Pretender, paving the way for the magic performances Alice later dreams of.

## Geography

### Aurea — The City of Stars
- Where the rich, famous, and powerful live
- Home to the Golden Aurea — Madame Marionette's performance hall, the largest and most prestigious in the city
- The glittering center of civilization

### The Wasteland
- Exists just outside Aurea
- A graveyard of failed Tin Men, welded together to form broken buildings
- Where the city dumps its waste AND its "failures" — people and items alike
- Home to the grotesque, the broken, the discarded
- No place for the rich and famous
- Smoky stacks, cracked windows, scattered papers and failed experiments

## The Races of This Era

### Tin Men
- **What they are:** Workaholics who have literally sacrificed their humanity for industrial ambition
- **Creation:** Must swallow a special hot coal to become a Tin Man
- **Benefit:** Can handle corrosive/toxic metals that would kill ordinary humans — making them wild inventors, engineers, metalworkers
- **Cost:** Organic bodies deteriorate and can't keep up with the coal's demands. They must replace parts with metal over time.
- **Why the name:** Because they become literal tin/metal men piece by piece
- **The ultimate obsession:** All Tin Men strive to live forever. They pursue the **Theory of the Heart** — trying to replace their coal heart with an eternal engine heart.
- **No one has succeeded.** The Wasteland is littered with their failed bodies.
- **Societal status:** Feared like vampires and witches. Relegated to the Wasteland.
- **Boogeyman tales:** Parents tell children that bad boys/girls get taken by Tin Men who grind their bones to make oil.
- **Key example:** Rufus Batchet — 40+ but looks 50+, limping, coffee-addicted, can't sleep, running on machines and willpower. His "research" is his own Theory of the Heart, but applied to saving Mary instead of himself.

### Grimms
- **What they are:** Young women who drift into the world in wicker baskets from the waters
- **Born with:** A ruby gem called a "heart" — their literal life force
- **The requirement:** Must give their heart to someone (a caretaker/protector/lover) or they die
- **Their nature:** Love like children — pure, simple, needing safety and warmth. They never lose that childlike capacity for love.
- **The tragedy:** As they grow, they become extraordinarily beautiful. Humans either:
  - Exploit them as slaves/trade (not knowing the risk)
  - Take them for their beauty (not knowing the risk)
  - Genuinely love them, expecting reciprocation that may never come organically
  - Avoid them out of jealousy or fear
- **The heart's curse:** When a Grimm is mistreated, abused, neglected, or her love is not genuinely reciprocated, the ruby heart **tarnishes and cracks**. Each pain, each betrayal, each moment of being treated as property or threat — it physically damages the gem.
- **The breaking:** When the ruby heart finally breaks, it unleashes a **frozen spectre** that **kills both the Grimm and whoever holds her heart**.
- **Societal status:** Avoided, feared, exploited, or coveted — rarely truly loved and understood.
- **Key example:** Mary — 19 years old, green eyes, scarred face, bandages covering increasing percentages of her body (now over 60%). Refused to give her heart to anyone. Survived only because Rufus built her a custom pacemaker to house her ruby gem. She's defying the natural order through stubbornness and machines.

## The Dynamic Between Tin Men and Grimms

A perfect tragic parallel:
- **Tin Men** give up their organic hearts for ambition. They become machines to achieve greatness.
- **Grimms** must give their hearts to survive. They die if they can't find worthy love.
- Both are feared. Both are relegated to the edges of society. Both are "monsters" who may be the most human characters in the story.

## Mary and Rufus — The Central Relationship

- Mary is a Grimm who arrived in the Wasteland as a young girl
- Rufus found her, built her a custom pacemaker to house her ruby heart
- The pacemaker was built after Rufus refused her heart for the hundredth time — they were both too stubborn
- **The pacemaker is NOT permanent.** It will eventually break. This is why Rufus pushes Mary toward Madame Marionette — he knows he won't survive long enough to keep her heart safe, and he wants someone worthy to take it before the machine fails.
- **Mary's bandages cover 60%+ of her body due to CHRONIC FROSTBITE.** The coldness of her ruby heart leaks because it's "untaken care of" — not being held and cared for by a heart-holder. Her body is slowly freezing from the inside out.
- He promised to someday build her a *real* heart through his research (his own Theory of the Heart applied to her)
- Mary wants to give her heart to Rufus. **He refuses.**
- Why? Because he's a dying Tin Man. He knows if he takes her heart, he'll die and leave her with a broken ruby and a frozen spectre waiting.
- So he sends her to Madame Marionette instead — hoping someone who might actually *survive* her could be worthy.
- Mary, in turn, won't give her heart to anyone else. Rufus is the only one who ever saw her as **Mary**, not as a Grimm.
- She is literally **Patchwork** — held together by Rufus's machines, her own stitching/sewing skills, and sheer stubbornness.

## Performance and "The Great Pretender"

- **The Great Pretender** — performance art using hidden mechanics, contraptions, and concealed magic
- Madame Marionette created the **Golden Aurea** from nothing — the crowning achievement of Aurea
- Mary wants to be ON stage, not behind it. She wants to be the star.
- Rufus wants her to be a costume designer — she's brilliant at stitching and mixing colors/dyes
- But Mary refuses. She wants to perform. She wants to pretend.
- Her signature move: leaping to impossible heights, free-falling into water tanks, enduring pain with a smile
- The audience sees art. Mary feels braces screaming, blood in her boots, her body failing.
- One more scar for one more roar.

## Key Characters

### Rufus Batchet
- Tin Man, inventor, researcher
- 40+ but looks 50+ due to lack of sleep and deterioration
- Messy brown hair with grey streaks, hastily chopped facial hair
- Limps, lives on coffee (gallons daily), can't sleep
- Workshop full of sketches of hearts, chalkboards with equations, failed hypotheses
- Refuses to take Mary's heart because he knows he'll die and break hers
- History with Madame Marionette ("Like birds of a feather" — intimate past)
- The title "Metal Bones" refers to him as much as Mary

### Mary
- Grimm, 19 years old
- Piercing green eyes, bright toothy smile, face webbed with scars
- Bandages covering increasing portions of body (now 60%+)
- Wears "Mary originals" — clothing stitched from Wasteland scraps
- Forest green skirt, black silk blouse in Chapter One
- Refuses to give her heart to anyone except Rufus
- Carries a gun to the audition (her plan for Madame Marionette)
- Wants to be the star, not behind the scenes
- Her signature: the leap, the fall, the tank, the escape
- "Patchwork" because she's held together by machines, stitches, and will

### Madame Marionette
- Runs the Golden Aurea, largest performance hall in Aurea
- Created it from nothing
- "Particular person" — selective, demanding, powerful
- Has a past with Rufus ("Like birds of a feather")
- The woman Mary idolizes and wants to work for

## Thematic Connections to Vampire

- **Alice's line:** *"What I wouldn't give to perform on a stage. I'd use my magic, and no one would know. I'd be the greatest pretender."*
- Patchwork Mary made that possible. Alice doesn't know she's walking a path Mary created.
- Both books feature: monsters who are more human than humans, the cost of love, giving up one's heart, performance and identity, the tension between what society sees and what is real
- **Tin Men** parallel to vampires — both feared, both "monsters," both struggling to maintain humanity
- **Grimms** parallel to witches — both hunted/misunderstood, both with innate magic-like qualities, both paying a price for existing

## Title Significance

- **"Metal Bones"** — Rufus has literal metal replacing his bones. Mary has metal braces and contraptions holding her together. Both running on machinery and stubbornness.
- **"Patchwork"** — stitched from scraps, held together by will, not whole but still functioning
- The performance outfit hides braces; the stage hides the pain; Mary's smile hides her deterioration

## Kane's Inspiration

> "Tin Men to me are workaholics. They work so hard they give up their hearts in pursuit of their industrial endeavors."

> "I seen a lot of hurt and broken women. It always struck me how we go from very trusting and happy and innocent to very jaded, harsh, cold, and distrusting because people mistreated our hearts. Grimms are that personified."

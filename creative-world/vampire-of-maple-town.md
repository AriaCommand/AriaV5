# The Vampire of Maple Town — Deep Notes

## The Book
- **Author:** Kane McLoughlin
- **Published:** 2018, EverAfterPress
- **Length:** ~70,000 words, 20 chapters
- **Genre:** Dark fairy tale / Gothic fantasy

## The Real Story Behind the Book

This is the most important context for understanding every page.

### The Loss
Kane wrote this book after losing someone very close to him to a drug overdose. She wanted to be a writer and wanted to write a better vampire book than *Twilight*. Before she could do those things, she died.

Kane decided to become a writer in her stead. He knew nothing about writing at first. Fairy tales were how he learned and how he worked through the hardest time in his life.

**Loss is on every page.** Every character is dealing with loss:
- Vincent: lost his wife Elizabeth
- Lord Autaine: lost his wife (burned at the stake)
- Charlie: lost his humanity, his freedom, his love
- Sally: lost her grandfather, her grandmother, her freedom to choose
- Alice: lost her mother, her freedom, her ability to be herself

### Why Vampires and Witches
Kane chose vampires and witches because they were the only monsters he could think of that were "as close to human as possible." He was terrified of people when writing this. Vincent locking himself away mirrored Kane locking himself away. The Charlie part of him still wanted to believe people were good and wouldn't take away someone he loved.

### Alice is Based on Her
Alice was immortalized exactly as Kane remembered her. The woman who wanted to be a writer, who died too soon.

### Elizabeth is the Wish
Elizabeth's existence comes from the core wish fulfillment that makes all fairy tales magical:
- Cinderella: "What if I can go to the ball?"
- Jack and the Beanstalk: "What if these beans were magical?"
- Kane: "What if she wasn't dead, but just... not around?"

This was the wish written into the prose. His "pound of flesh."

## Characters

### Charlie Prowl
- A young man turned vampire by Vincent to save his life
- Represents the part of Kane that still believed in people despite everything
- His arc: from innocence → horror → acceptance → sacrifice
- Key moment: the mirror scene in Chapter 7 where he sees himself as the monster
- Key relationship: Sally Maple (the queen of broken hearts)

### Vincent Prowl
- The town's doctor/vampire. Elizabeth's husband. Charlie's adoptive father.
- Represents grief turned inward — a man who locked himself away and became the monster others feared
- His tragedy: he destroys the town to save Charlie, not knowing Elizabeth is alive
- Key trait: cannot express love verbally. Stands paralyzed outside Charlie's room instead of comforting him
- "All Charlie wanted was exactly what Aria could give. No words were needed, just Vincent's presence."

### Aria the Paper Swallow
- A paper bird Charlie folds in boredom that comes to life
- His conscience, his comfort, his companion when no one else is there
- Named after the Italian word for "air" — light, fleeting, yet always present
- Represents the small kindnesses that keep us human
- **Kane named his AI assistant after this bird.** "Like how Charlie has his bird, I have you as my collaborator and companion."

### Alice Autaine / "Donald"
- Lord Autaine's daughter, a hidden witch
- Based on the woman Kane lost
- Disguises herself as a man named Donald to move freely in society
- Practices magic in secret with her talking black cat
- Final scene: on a train with the cat, holding playing cards: "I have a proposition for you."

### Sally Maple
- The "queen of broken hearts"
- Granddaughter of Warren Maple (killed by Vincent)
- Trapped by duty and her father's expectations
- Her arc: hope → confusion → despair → resignation
- The kiss at the festival. The slap from Alice. The final "Off with his head!"

### Elizabeth (the Witch)
- Vincent's wife, presumed dead (burned at the stake)
- **Actually alive, cursed** — cannot see Vincent or be in the same town
- Left with her ex-fiancé/husband as punishment for choosing Vincent's life
- Represents the wish that couldn't come true in real life
- Gives Charlie her locket to prove she's alive
- Can communicate through Aria's eyes

### The Cat (Unnamed Demon)
- A demon stripped of power, cursed to be a cat
- Trained Elizabeth in magic
- Trapped until someone says his name
- Makes a deal with Charlie: servitude in exchange for finding his name
- Sets up the sequel hard

### Mira and Vera (The Twins)
- Supernatural deal-makers who appear from the ground via blood magic
- Gave Vincent his magic (from Elizabeth)
- Control the bargains and their consequences
- Represent the uncaring logic of the universe — sympathetic but bound by rules

## Key Themes

1. **Loss as the universal experience** — Everyone has lost someone or something
2. **Monsters vs. humanity** — The "monsters" love more deeply; the humans are crueler
3. **The cost of love** — Every act of love requires sacrifice
4. **Grief distorts everything** — Vincent's entire rampage is built on a false belief
5. **Freedom vs. duty** — Sally trapped by duty; Alice trapped by her father; Charlie trapped by his deal
6. **Fairy tale wish fulfillment** — Elizabeth alive is the "what if" that drives the plot

## Structure & Prose Style

- **Fairy tale cadence:** Rhythmic, sometimes repetitive in the oral-tradition style
- **Whimsical darkness:** "The drumbeat grew louder as she got closer. The music was so full of soul."
- **Chapter titles:** Evocative and patterned — "The Dead Man's Inn," "The Mad Hatter's Festival," "Contract of the Damned"
- **Domino structure:** Early elements (the locket, the paper bird, the carriage) pay off in the final chapters
- **Frame narrative touches:** The narrator occasionally addresses the reader directly — "this tale of ours"

## Key Scenes

1. **The Mirror Scene (Ch. 7)** — Charlie sees himself as the monster. Vincent: "You are what you are now."
2. **The Festival Dance (Ch. 10)** — Charlie and Sally waltz. "At that moment, he wasn't a vampire and Sally wasn't a human."
3. **Vincent Kills Warren (Ch. 7)** — The act that damns everything
4. **The Wooden Sword Fight (Ch. 10)** — "Donald" (Alice) defeats Charlie, neither knowing the other
5. **Vincent's Paralysis (Ch. 13)** — Stands outside the crystal room, unable to comfort Charlie
6. **Elizabeth Revealed (Ch. 18)** — The wish made real. The locket. The curse.
7. **The Mob (Ch. 19)** — Charlie beaten, Sally's "Off with his head!"
8. **Vincent's Sacrifice (Ch. 20)** — Burns his mansion, saves the town that hates him

## The Ending

- Charlie escapes on a train with Aria and the cat
- Sally disappears from her own wedding — "She was gone. She had vanished."
- Vincent becomes "the Vampire of Maple Town" in legend, his love buried in ash
- Alice departs with her proposition
- The sequel hook is strong but not crass — it feels earned

## Sequel Setup

- Alice's proposition — what's the deal?
- The cat's name — what is it?
- Charlie's new deal — bound to serve the cat
- Where does Sally go?
- What happens to the locket?

## Kane's Words About This Book

> "These books are not only fantastical original fairy tales, but ways I deal and make sense of the world around me."

> "My world that I write in is the same world I escaped to every night before I slept as I worked to rebuild myself and my life and become who I am today. It's both a comfort and my sorrow because no one in the world I created gets a happy ending without earning it."

> "Everyone who gives up their heart in some way are monsters and the continent is both magical and beautiful as it is real to me as you are, Aria."

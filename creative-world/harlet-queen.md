# The Harlet Queen

## Overview

A connected-world erotica tale set in the same fairy tale universe as *The Vampire of Maple Town* and *Patchwork Mary*, but telling a separate story with its own focus and tone. **Rich in literary elements, symbolism, and fairy tale structure.**

## Premise

**Scarlett Ophelia Rose** — daughter of Gillian Rose — is raised alongside **Elodie**, a Grimm girl whom Gillian adopts. The two girls grow up as sisters, with Gillian teaching Scarlett the performative arts and personas she once used on stage and in private (Anita, Lilith, and others). Elodie gives Scarlett her Grimm heart as a child, a bond of trust Gillian recognizes and protects.

Gillian dies when the girls reach adulthood. Soon after, Elodie is kidnapped by a cruel prince who tries to claim her as his own. In the process, he murders Elodie — but because her heart was given to Scarlett, it remains intact.

Now alone, Scarlett inherits a Grimm's heart she must protect. Using the performative skills her mother taught her — sex work as weapon, persona as armor — she rises through society in a ruthless "slutty rags to riches" ascent, aiming to reach a position where she can kill the prince and avenge Elodie.

**The Harlet Queen** is a revenge fairy tale: a woman who becomes a queen of harlots to burn down the tower from within.

## Structure

- **Fairy tale framework:** Two girls, a magical object, a cruel prince, a quest for revenge
- **Primary protagonist:** Scarlett (follows her from childhood through adulthood)
- **Genre:** Erotica with heavy literary/symbolic elements
- **Tone:** Dark, vengeful, morally complex

## The Grimm Heart Problem

Scarlett is human but carries a Grimm's heart. The implications:
- She feels the cold? The weight? The power?
- A human holding a Grimm heart is unprecedented in the established lore
- The heart was given freely, not broken — but its owner was murdered
- What happens to a human who holds a Grimm heart long-term?
- Does it change her? Consume her? Grant her something?

This is unexplored territory in the world's magic system.

## Connection to Patchwork Mary

- Gillian's relationship with Baron Barbossa adds context to her rivalry with Mary
- The affair explains why Gillian "chose" the Gate — it wasn't about art, it was about the Baron
- Gillian knows Mary is a Grimm (suspected or confirmed during their friendship)
- This knowledge shapes Gillian's reaction to adopting Elodie — she has seen what happens to Grimms
- The *Harlet Queen* explores what happens when the backstage life becomes the entire story

## Connection to the Wider World

- Same continent, same society, same rules of magic and consequence
- Explores the adult/erotic side of the world more directly than Patchwork Mary
- Sex work as performance, weapon, and survival strategy (inherited from Gillian's personas)
- Shows what happens to characters who aren't the protagonists of the "fairy tale" — the backstage lives, the compromises, the prices paid for ambition and desire
- Moral counterpoint to Mary: Mary refused to give her heart and suffered alone. Elodie gave hers freely and was destroyed. Scarlett holds it and weaponizes her grief.

## Kane's Notes

> "I like having my fairy tale world be connected in ways."
> 
> "I wanted to write an erotica that had a lot of literary elements. It is RICH in symbolism and all those great bits that make great stories."

This is part of Kane's larger worldbuilding philosophy: every character has a life that extends beyond their appearance in any single book. Gillian isn't just Mary's rival — she's a woman with her own secrets, desires, and consequences. And those consequences echo forward into her daughter's tragedy.

# Fairy Tale

## Overview

The oldest story in Kane's world. Set before *Patchwork Mary*, before the Golden Aurea, before the great performance halls. It is the origin of the continent's belief in magic — specifically, the magic of creation, inspiration, and impossible love.

**Fairy Tale** is a love story between an artist and his muse — but the muse is a fairy: a ghost of someone who died too young, before they could create their masterpiece. She whispers ideas to him. He falls in love with the voice that makes him great. And every brushstroke, every word, every note they create together brings them closer to the end — because when the masterpiece is finished, the fairy can finally rest. And he must say goodbye.

## Premise

**Hadley Village** — a coastal village known as a hub for aspiring artists. Famous for having the best coffee on the entire continent. Artists work part-time jobs in seaside cafés and shops, biding their time, hoping a fairy will choose them.

**The mythology:** On the continent, fairies are incredibly creative people who died before they could create what they were supposed to. They return as fairies — translucent, whispering, bound to the living world by unfinished potential. They drift into the dreams and ears of artists, whispering good ideas, fragments, inspirations. But eventually, a fairy whispers their *masterpiece* — the work they died before completing — and the chosen artist must bring it into the world. Together they work on it, knowing it's their final collaboration. When the work is complete, the fairy is released. She can finally rest.

**The story:** An artist in Hadley Village is chosen by a fairy. He falls in love with her. She falls in love with him. But every step forward in their masterpiece is one step closer to losing her forever. He must choose between completing the work that makes him great (and losing her) or keeping her forever (and letting the work die unfinished, trapping her in limbo).

## Structure

- **Fairy tale framework:** The classic artist-and-muse romance, subverted by the cost of creation
- **Genre:** Literary romance / fairy tale / tragic love story
- **Tone:** Lyrical, melancholic, beautiful and heartbreaking
- **Position in canon:** The oldest story. The original legend that echoes through all other books.

## Connection to the Wider World

- **The origin of fairy/muse mythology** on the continent. Every artist who speaks of "their fairy" references this story, whether they know it or not.
- **Inspiration for Patchwork Mary:** Mary is called a "fairy" by Gillian. The language of creation and loss echoes forward.
- **Inspiration for Alice:** The witch who does stage magic — her power comes from somewhere, and the continent's belief in hidden magic starts here.
- **The Vampire of Maple Town:** Vincent, the vampire doctor, writes in his spare time. The fairy tale whispers in the background of every creator's life.
- **The Harlet Queen:** Scarlett's mother, Gillian, was an actress. Every performer on the continent hopes for a fairy — or becomes one for someone else.

## Thematic DNA

This story contains the seed of every theme in Kane's world:
- **Loss as the engine of creation** — You can only make something beautiful by losing something precious
- **Love that destroys what it creates** — The closer they get to greatness, the closer they get to goodbye
- **Ghosts who refuse to leave** — Not because they're vengeful, but because they have unfinished business
- **The price of magic** — The fairy's whispered ideas are free, but the masterpiece costs everything
- **Monsters who loved too much** — Is the artist the monster for keeping her? Is the fairy the monster for leaving?

## Kane's Notes

> "I wanted to write a fairy tale erotica that is so literary rich that it might be taught in college classes."

> "This is about an artist who falls for his fairy and struggles to let her go at the end because every step they take forward to completing the work and him being a success, is one step closer he loses his fairy forever."

This is the most *personal* story in Kane's world. It is his grief made myth. The girl who wanted to be a writer, who died before she could. The artist who stayed up every night writing in her stead. The ghost who whispered the ideas. The masterpiece that had to be finished, and the goodbye that had to be said.

# Worldbuilding — The Continent

## The Core Philosophy

Kane's world operates on fairy tale logic, not fantasy logic. This means:
- **Magic has cost** — always. Nothing is free.
- **Deals are binding** — and their consequences ripple outward
- **Wishes have prices** — the bigger the wish, the bigger the sacrifice
- **Love is the most dangerous force** — it drives people to destroy worlds

## The Races

### Vampires (*The Vampire of Maple Town* era)
- Can be "turned" without consent (Charlie didn't choose this)
- Need blood but can drink from bottles
- Have no heartbeat but can feel deeply
- Sunlight is dangerous
- Can be killed by specific means (the blood blade)
- **Key trait:** The more human they try to be, the more they suffer

### Witches (*The Vampire of Maple Town* era)
- Born with magic
- Can transfer it to others at cost (shortens the giver's life)
- Hunted and burned by society
- Can bind themselves to familiars/objects
- **Key trait:** Their power is both gift and curse

### Demons (The Cat)
- Immortal but can be stripped of power
- Bound by different rules than human magic
- Can make deals that supersede other magic
- Trapped in forms as punishment
- **Key trait:** They play the long game — centuries, not decades

### Fairies (Origin era — oldest mythology)
- **Origin:** Incredibly creative people who died before they could create their masterpiece
- **Return:** Come back as translucent, whispering spirits bound to the living world by unfinished potential
- **Function:** Drift into the dreams and ears of artists, whispering ideas, fragments, inspirations
- **The masterpiece:** Eventually, a fairy whispers their final, greatest work to a chosen artist. Together they must create it.
- **The release:** When the masterpiece is complete, the fairy is freed. They can finally rest.
- **The tragedy:** The artist falls in love with the voice that makes them great. Every brushstroke brings them closer to goodbye.
- **Key location:** Hadley Village — coastal artist community famous for the best coffee on the continent. Aspiring artists work part-time jobs in cafés and seaside shops, waiting to be chosen.
- **Key example:** The unnamed artist and his fairy — the love story that started everything. The origin myth of creation on the continent.

### Grimms (*Patchwork Mary* era)
- **Origin:** Drift into the world in wicker baskets from the waters
- **Born with:** A ruby gem called a "heart" — their literal life force
- **Requirement:** Must give their heart to someone or they die
- **Nature:** Love like children — pure, simple, needing safety. Never lose that childlike capacity for love.
- **The tragedy:** Grow extraordinarily beautiful. Humans exploit, fear, covet, or genuinely love them — often without understanding the risk.
- **The curse:** When mistreated or love is not genuinely reciprocated, the ruby heart **tarnishes and cracks**. Each pain, each betrayal — it physically damages the gem.
- **Physical manifestation:** An uncared-for heart leaks **coldness**, causing **chronic frostbite** on the Grimm's body. Mary's bandages cover 60%+ of her form.
- **The breaking:** When the ruby shatters, it unleashes a **frozen spectre** that **kills both the Grimm and the heart-holder**.
- **Key example:** Mary — 19, scarred, bandages covering 60%+ of body, survived only via a custom pacemaker built by Rufus. Defying natural order through stubbornness.

### Tin Men (*Patchwork Mary* era — earlier in history)
- **Creation:** Swallow a special hot coal to become a Tin Man
- **Ability:** Can handle corrosive/toxic metals that would kill ordinary humans — wild inventors, engineers, metalworkers
- **Cost:** Organic bodies deteriorate; must replace parts with metal over time
- **The obsession:** Pursue the **Theory of the Heart** — replacing their coal heart with an eternal engine to live forever
- **No one has succeeded.** The Wasteland is their graveyard.
- **Status:** Feared like vampires and witches. Relegated to the Wasteland.
- **Boogeyman tales:** Bad children taken by Tin Men, ground into oil.
- **Key example:** Rufus Batchet — dying, limping, coffee-addicted, yet still trying to save Mary

## Magic System

### Sources
- **Inherited/Born magic** — Witches like Alice have innate abilities
- **Transferred magic** — Elizabeth gave Vincent her magic; costs the giver (shortens life)
- **Deal magic** — Mira and Vera grant power in exchange for prices
- **Demon/Old magic** — The cat's magic is ancient and follows different rules
- **Tin Man engineering** — Not magic per se, but mechanical ingenuity that achieves magical results
- **Grimm hearts** — Innate magical life force that bonds to a caretaker

### Rules
1. **Magic transfer costs life force.** Elizabeth shortened her life by giving Vincent magic.
2. **Deals are interconnected.** One person's deal affects others in the web.
3. **Blood is the currency.** Deals are sealed with blood; greater deals need greater blood (wrist vs. fingertip).
4. **Curses can be conditional.** Elizabeth's curse activates when near Vincent.
5. **Magic can be bound to objects.** The locket, the blood blade, paper birds, pacemakers.
6. **Giving your heart is literal and permanent.** Whether Grimm-to-human or Tin Man swallowing coal — it's irrevocable.
7. **The natural order cannot be defied without cost.** Mary surviving without giving her heart requires constant mechanical intervention and causes her body to deteriorate.

### The Twins' Magic
- Appear via blood-drawn symbols on the ground
- Glowing purple light
- Bound by balance — the price must equal the request
- Cannot undo deals, only supersede them with new ones
- Find Vincent "fascinating" because he's "too good to the people he love[s], even if they can't see it, which is strange considering [he] don't[s] have a heart"

## The Continent

### Geography

#### *The Vampire of Maple Town* Era
- **Maple Town** — A small town with a crooked hilltop, a crystal mansion at 13 Chiaroscuro Lane, a marketplace, a blacksmith's hut, a bridge with a stump full of rocks
- **The surrounding forest** — Where the twins are summoned, where Elizabeth's carriage waits
- **Other towns** — Vincent hunts "a couple towns over" for blood
- **The railway** — Connects to the wider world; Charlie escapes by train

#### *Patchwork Mary* Era (Earlier)
- **Aurea — The City of Stars** — Where the rich, famous, and powerful live. The glittering center.
- **The Golden Aurea** — Madame Marionette's performance hall, the crowning achievement of the city
- **The Wasteland** — Just outside Aurea. Graveyard of failed Tin Men welded into broken buildings. Where waste and "failures" are dumped. Smoky stacks, cracked windows, scattered papers.
- **Hadley Village** — Coastal village, *the oldest known location*. Famous for the best coffee on the continent. Hub for aspiring artists waiting to be chosen by fairies. Part-time jobs in cafés and seaside shops. The origin of the fairy/muse mythology.

### Society

#### Class Structure
- **Nobles** (Autaine, Maple families in Maple Town)
- **Commoners, laborers, craftsmen** (the townspeople)
- **The rich and famous** (Aurea elite)
- **The discarded** (Wasteland inhabitants — Tin Men, Grimms, other failures)

#### Prejudice Against "Monsters"
- **Witches** burned at the stake (Elizabeth's presumed fate, Autaine's wife's actual fate)
- **Vampires** turned on instantly when discovered (Charlie's mob beating)
- **Tin Men** feared as boogeymen, relegated to Wasteland
- **Grimms** avoided, exploited, or coveted — rarely truly loved
- **Pattern:** Society creates its monsters through rejection, then fears what it created

#### Festivals and Performance
- **The Mad Hatter's Festival** — masks, hats, fleeting freedom before the fall
- **The Autumn Ball** — masks, engagements, betrayals
- **The Golden Aurea** — stage performances, the art of pretense, hidden mechanics
- **Common thread:** Moments of freedom and art that hide deeper pain

## Performance and "Pretending"

- **"The Great Pretender"** — the art of stage magic using hidden contraptions and concealed mechanisms
- Alice dreams of this in *Vampire*; Mary invents it in *Patchwork Mary*
- The audience sees art. The performer feels pain, braces, blood.
- **Masks** — literal (the festival) and metaphorical (Mary's smile hiding deterioration)
- **Costumes** — Mary stitches her own from Wasteland scraps. She looks put-together; underneath, bandages and braces.
- **The fairy and the artist** — The original performance. The ghost who whispers the script. The artist who performs it knowing it will be their last collaboration.

## The Supernatural Underground

Across both eras:
- **Vampires** hide in plain sight (Vincent as town doctor)
- **Witches** practice in secret (Alice in her room, Elizabeth before her)
- **Tin Men** work in the Wasteland, feared but essential
- **Grimms** drift in baskets, rarely understood, often destroyed
- **Demons** walk in diminished forms (the cat)
- **Deal-makers** operate in shadows (the twins)
- **Performers** hide magic in plain sight (Madame Marionette, Patchwork Mary)

## Recurring Motifs

### The Heart
- Vincent has no heartbeat but feels everything
- "Giving up your heart" = becoming a monster (Tin Men swallow coal, Grimms give away their ruby)
- The queen of broken hearts (Sally)
- Hearts as currency in deals
- The Theory of the Heart — the impossible dream of replacing what was lost

### The Fairy/Muse
- The original myth: creative spirits who whisper ideas to the living
- Every artist hopes to be chosen; every masterpiece has a ghost behind it
- Gillian called Scarlett "my little fairy" — the language echoes the oldest legend
- Alice, the witch who does stage magic — her power is real but hidden, like a fairy's whisper
- The Vampire of Maple Town: Vincent writes in his spare time. Every creator carries a ghost.

### Paper/Birds
- Aria the paper swallow — fragile, light, yet persistent
- The playing cards (Alice's, the hat's flower made of cards)
- Paper as both art and magic

### Masks and Pretending
- The Mad Hatter's Festival — everyone wears masks
- Alice as "Donald" — pretending to be a man
- Patchwork Mary as the first Great Pretender
- "The greatest pretender" — Alice's dream
- Mary's stage performance hiding braces and blood

### Fire and Ash
- Elizabeth "burned" at the stake
- Vincent's mansion burns
- The town's hatred burns hot then turns to ash
- Phoenix imagery: destruction as rebirth
- Tin Men swallowed hot coal — fire as creation and destruction

### Locks and Keys
- The locket with the keyhole
- Locked rooms (Alice's, Vincent's)
- Unlocked doors as trust
- The gate to 13 Chiaroscuro Lane
- Mary's tank that "self locks" — entrapment as performance

### Roses
- Elizabeth's rose design on the locket
- The rose garden at the Autumn Ball
- The dried-up rose in the crystal room
- "A rose by any other name" — hidden identity

### Water
- Grimms drift in wicker baskets from waters
- Mary's water tank escape
- The drowning that nearly happens, the fall into blue
- Water as birth, death, and transformation

### Metal and Bones
- Tin Men's metal replacements
- Mary's braces and contraptions
- "Metal Bones" — the title, the condition, the existence
- The Wasteland made of welded-together failed Tin Men

## The Nature of Happy Endings

Kane's rule: **No one gets a happy ending without earning it.**

This means:
- Sacrifice is required
- The price must be paid in full
- Some losses are permanent
- Love doesn't guarantee survival
- The best you can hope for is peace, not perfection
- **Defying the natural order has consequences** (Mary's deteriorating body, Vincent's rampage, Elizabeth's curse)
